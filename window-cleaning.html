Window cleaning: the basics

What the job involves

Most window cleaners now use water-fed poles with purified water from the ground, which reaches upper floors without ladders and leaves glass to dry clear. They clean frames and sills too, and many also clean conservatory roofs, fascias and gutters.

What to ask a pro, and what to have ready

Say how many windows and storeys and how often you want them done (four to eight weekly is common). Ask how they take payment, whether they need access to the back garden, and whether inside cleaning is available.

Safety and good to know

LincsDirectory doesn't vet or endorse businesses — always check credentials and get a written quote.

Good to know

purified water cleaning leaves windows wet but they dry without spots, so don't worry if they look streaky at first. Tell the cleaner about any windows that are fragile, leaded or stained. If you want frames and sills included each time, agree it up front. If the cleaner uses ladders rather than poles, ask how they work safely and whether they are insured for working at height.

About window cleaning

Do I need to be home for the window cleaner?

Usually not, as long as they can reach all the windows. Many cleaners text the day before and take payment online or by bank transfer.
