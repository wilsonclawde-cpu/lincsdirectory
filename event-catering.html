Event catering: the basics

What the job involves

Caterers provide buffets, hog roasts, sit-down meals, afternoon teas and street-food style service for weddings, parties, funerals and business events, either delivered or served on site with staff. Many cafés and restaurants also cater locally.

What to ask a pro, and what to have ready

Give the date, numbers, venue and the style you want, with any dietary needs. Ask for a menu and price per head in writing, what is included (staff, crockery, clearing up), deposit and cancellation terms, and their food hygiene rating.

Safety and good to know

Food businesses are inspected and rated under the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme; you can look up a caterer's rating on the Food Standards Agency website. LincsDirectory doesn't vet or endorse businesses — always check credentials and get a written quote.

Good to know

caterers usually need access to the venue in advance, a power supply and somewhere to prepare and clear. Agree final numbers a week or two before the event and ask how late changes are handled. For outdoor events, ask about marquees, generators and licensing for a bar.

About event catering

How far ahead should I book a caterer?

Weddings and large events are often booked months ahead; a buffet for a party can usually be arranged with a few weeks' notice. Popular summer weekends go first.
